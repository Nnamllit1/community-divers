## 3. Donations and permitted paid benefits

You may operate a service without charge. Funding connected to the covered service
is permitted as genuine voluntary donations, disclosed shared hosting-cost
contributions, or optional purely cosmetic ranks, titles and profile badges,
subject to sections 4 and 5. You may also recover the actual cost of supplying a

disguising service charges as distribution fees.

**Genuine donations are unlimited.** There is no limit under this license on their
receipt, retention, use for service costs or retention as operator income. They do
not consume or increase the paid-revenue allowance. This does not remove any
separate restriction the operator agreed with the donor.

A genuine donation must not be required or expected in exchange for an entitlement.
A simple thank-you is permitted with the donor's consent. A payment that purchases
a promised rank, badge, priority, access or another benefit is paid revenue, even
if called a donation. Do not split a compulsory payment into a nominal purchase
and a supposed donation to avoid the cap. A genuinely optional separate gift may
be zero without changing the purchased benefit or its availability.

Permitted paid ranks, titles and badges must be decorative only. They must not
affect stats, equipment, progression, rewards, visibility to enemy AI, mission
rules, campaign influence, moderation/administrative permissions or gameplay
commands. They must not buy queue priority, reserved slots or exclusive gameplay.
Shared hosting-cost contributions may fund ordinary access on disclosed,
equivalent terms, without a higher gameplay tier. All such paid receipts count
toward the same monthly revenue cap.

Paid pets and other kinds of cosmetics remain a possible future permission; this
version permits only the decorative ranks, titles and profile badges above.
It does not authorize advertising/sponsorship placements, paid equipment,
progression, mission outcomes or privileged campaign influence.

## 4. Paid-revenue cap and hosting buffer

Use a consistent calendar month and currency. "Eligible Costs" are actual,
documented costs reasonably attributable to operating the service during that
month: hosting, compute, bandwidth, storage, backups, domains, required
infrastructure services, payment-processing charges and directly attributable
transaction taxes. For owned equipment and shared internet/electricity, count only
a reasonable, documented service share over its useful life or billing period.
Prorate annual or prepaid infrastructure costs across the period they cover.
Do not count the same expense twice or allocate it to multiple services. Deduct
supplier refunds, credits and reimbursements for the same expense. Donations that
fund an expense do not erase that expense or count as extra costs.

Unpaid or imputed labor, operator compensation, unrelated purchases, development
budgets, personal income tax and inflated related-party charges are not Eligible
Costs. Related-party expenses count only at actual attributable cost, with no
markup. Paying yourself must not increase the hosting-cost base used for the cap.

**Paid revenue in a calendar month must not exceed twice that month's Eligible
Costs.** Paid revenue means the receipts for paid ranks, titles, badges, shared
access contributions and any equivalent payments, after actual customer refunds.
Taxes are included in receipts and recognized once as eligible expenses where
applicable; do not deduct the same tax twice. Genuine donations are excluded.
Count commonly controlled covered services together; moving money or invoices
between them cannot enlarge the allowance. Do not borrow unused allowance from
another month. Reconcile against actual costs and refund any excess paid revenue
within 30 days after month-end; reduce or suspend sales to avoid repeated excess.

For example, EUR 100 in monthly Eligible Costs permits EUR 200 in monthly paid
revenue, leaving EUR 100 after those costs. Four months at that level can build
EUR 400 in savings, enough to cover another four months of EUR 100 hosting costs
without further sales. This assumes the operator saves the margin and costs stay
at that level; it is not a guarantee of funding.

An operator may retain the permitted margin as income or save it for future
service costs. The cap limits paid receipts for the month, not the accumulated
balance of lawfully retained savings. Spending existing savings is not new
revenue. A genuine donation can add any amount to those savings or to operator
income, without reducing the paid allowance. An above-cap sale cannot be relabeled
as a gift or placed in a reserve to avoid the refund requirement.

## 5. Prohibited arrangements and transparency

Do not operate the covered service to offer real-money gambling, paid randomized
rewards, wagering with purchased or redeemable value, or cash-out schemes. Ordinary
random gameplay rewards without purchased stakes or cash-out are not gambling
under this condition. Do not sell gameplay advantages or benefits excluded by
section 3.

Do not arrange prohibited or above-cap sales indirectly through donation tiers,
affiliated stores, third parties, unrelated memberships or bundled purchases.
A payment that buys a benefit remains paid revenue regardless of its description.

Before collecting funding, publish the funding terms, paid benefits and cost
categories. Within 90 days after each calendar year, make an aggregate account
available to service users, with each month's paid receipts, refunds, eligible
costs and cap. Report genuine donations separately and disclose retained hosting
savings in aggregate. Keep supporting records for three years. Do not publish
donor identities, credentials or individual financial records. If a Licensor
identifies a specific credible compliance concern, provide reasonably necessary
redacted evidence or an independent accountant's confirmation, protecting personal
information. A donations-only service has no paid-revenue cap to calculate; it
need only identify that funding model and report aggregate donations.

## Worked funding examples

These examples explain the proposal; they do not make it effective.

| Situation | Result under this draft |
| --- | --- |
| EUR 100 monthly hosting; EUR 200 paid rank sales | Within the cap; EUR 100 remains after hosting and may be saved or retained. |
| Four months of EUR 100 costs and EUR 200 sales per month | EUR 400 in total margin; if saved, it funds four more months at EUR 100 without sales. |
| EUR 100 costs; EUR 200 sales; EUR 5,000 genuine donations | Sales are within the cap. All EUR 5,000 of donations may additionally be kept; they do not increase the paid-sales allowance. |
| EUR 100 costs; EUR 250 sales | EUR 50 must be refunded, even if the operator would prefer to keep it in a hosting reserve. |
| EUR 0 eligible costs; EUR 5,000 genuine donations | All donations are permitted; the paid-revenue allowance is zero. |
| Operator prepays EUR 1,200 for twelve months of hosting | EUR 100 monthly cost base for that service period; no EUR 2,400 sales spike in the purchase month. |
| Operator pays themselves for administration | This is not a hosting expense and cannot increase the paid-revenue cap. |
| Pay EUR 5 for a decorative profile badge | Permitted within the paid-revenue cap, with no gameplay or access advantage. |
| Donate EUR 5 to receive queue priority or stronger equipment | Prohibited; the label does not make the purchase a genuine donation. |
| Free pets for all players or ordinary random mission loot without bought stakes/cash-out | Not prohibited by these funding rules. |

A restricted license cannot guarantee that operators comply, retract earlier MIT
permissions, control independent implementations, or govern code a Licensor does
not have the rights to license. Review enforceability, scope and compatibility
before adopting this draft.
